Many Polish parents in the UK notice the same thing: their children understand Polish perfectly at five, start answering in English at seven, and by twelve are shy about speaking Polish at all. School, friends and almost everything on screen are in English. Polish television will not solve that on its own, but used well it is one of the easiest ways to keep the language alive at home.

## Why television helps

Children pick up language from hearing lots of it, in context, from voices other than their parents. At home they mostly hear Polish from mum, dad and perhaps grandparents on a video call, often about the same everyday topics. Cartoons, children's programmes and family films add:

- **New vocabulary** about animals, space, sport, feelings and stories, not just "brush your teeth".
- **Natural rhythm and humour**, the jokes and phrases their cousins in Poland use.
- **Motivation.** A favourite cartoon in Polish is a reason to listen that does not feel like homework.

## Making it work at home

**Make Polish the default for some viewing.** For example, weekend morning cartoons are always in Polish. A simple routine works better than a battle every evening.

**Watch together sometimes.** Ask about what happened, in Polish. Even short conversations turn passive listening into active speaking.

**Follow their interests.** If your child loves football, ski jumping or animal documentaries, find those in Polish. The topic matters more than the programme.

**Use subtitles for older children.** Polish audio with Polish subtitles helps reading as well as listening, which is useful for children attending Saturday Polish school.

**Keep it part of family life.** Watching a programme the grandparents are also watching in Poland gives everyone something to talk about on the next call.

## Setting it up safely

With IPTV, Polish TV reaches every screen in the house, so it is worth a few minutes on safety:

- **Use parental controls** in the player app where they are available, to lock adult categories behind a PIN.
- **Put children's viewing on the main TV**, or on a tablet used in a shared room, rather than behind a closed bedroom door.
- **Choose enough screens.** If children watch cartoons while a parent watches the news, a two-screen plan avoids arguments. Plans cover 1 to 4 screens at once.

## A realistic expectation

Television supports a language; it does not teach it alone. Children who hear Polish from family, speak it with friends and read a little in it as well as watching Polish programmes have the best chance of growing up confidently bilingual. That is a real advantage, in the UK and in Poland.
