For years, getting live sport and premium channels in the UK meant a satellite dish or a cable package, an engineer visit and a contract that ran for a year and a half. IPTV changes most of that. Here is a straight comparison, so you can decide which suits your household.

## Monthly cost

A UK satellite or cable package with sport and films typically runs from around £50 to £90 a month once all the add-ons are included, and that is before broadband. Introductory prices often rise when the deal ends.

IPTV UK costs £15 for a single month, £60 for 12 months (£5 a month) or £110 for 24 months (about £4.58 a month) on one screen. Even the four-screen 24-month plan works out at about £9 a month.

**Winner: IPTV**, by a wide margin.

## Contracts and price rises

Pay-TV contracts usually last 18 to 24 months, with fees if you leave early and annual price rises written into the terms.

With IPTV UK you pay once for the period you choose. Nothing renews automatically, the price is fixed for the plan you buy, and there is no exit fee.

**Winner: IPTV.**

## Installation

Satellite needs a dish on the outside wall, cabling and usually an engineer. Renters need the landlord's permission, and some flats ban dishes altogether. Cable needs a line to the property, which not every street has.

IPTV needs only broadband and a device. Install an app, log in and you are watching, typically within 15 minutes.

**Winner: IPTV.**

## Watching in more than one room

Pay-TV packages often charge extra each month for multiroom, and each room needs its own box.

An IPTV UK plan covers 1 to 4 devices at once: the living-room TV, a Fire TV Stick in the bedroom, a tablet in the kitchen and a phone on the move.

**Winner: IPTV.**

## Picture quality and reliability

Satellite has one real advantage: it does not depend on your broadband, so it is unaffected by a slow connection. On the other hand, heavy rain or snow can break up the picture.

IPTV quality depends on your connection. On a good fibre line it delivers Full HD and 4K comfortably. On a weak connection or patchy Wi-Fi it can buffer, which is usually fixable: see our [buffering guide](/blog/iptv-buffering-fixes) and [broadband speed guide](/blog/broadband-speed-for-iptv-uk).

**Winner: a draw.** Satellite wins on a poor connection, and IPTV wins on decent broadband and in bad weather.

## Moving house

With satellite you may need a new dish, a new installation or a transfer fee, and sometimes a new contract. With IPTV you log in at the new address.

**Winner: IPTV.**

## The verdict

If you have reasonable broadband (around 10 Mbps per screen for HD), IPTV UK is cheaper, more flexible and easier to live with than a traditional pay-TV package. Satellite still makes sense only where broadband is genuinely poor.
